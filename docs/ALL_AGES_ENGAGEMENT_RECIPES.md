## 📖 Engagement Recipes by Age

### Ages 3-8: Fun Recipe (Playful, Magical)

**Required Elements:**
1. **Silly Detail** - Funny sound, goofy rule, silly misunderstanding
2. **Magical Twist** - Object talks/sings/dances/glows/changes
3. **2+ Dialogue Lines** - Characters speak, objects talk
4. **Tiny Challenge** - Pattern, count, color choice, rhyme
5. **Mini Cliffhanger** - Sound appears, thing moves, light glows

**Example (Age 5):**
```
The door hums a low song—*mmmmm*—like it's thinking. You press your ear
close. Spot sniffs the bottom and sneezes. "Bless you!" the door giggles.
Wait, did the *door* just talk?

You notice a keyhole the size of your pinky. It glows soft purple.

Choices:
- Whisper "please open" ...and see if it answers
- Search for a tiny purple key ...that matches the glow
```

**Why it works**: Silly (sneeze), Magical (door talks), Dialogue ("Bless you!"), Challenge (find right key), Cliffhanger (glowing keyhole mystery)

---

### Ages 9-12: Adventure Recipe (Clever, Mysterious)

**Required Elements:**
1. **Clever Detail** - Ironic observation, unexpected insight, wordplay
2. **Mysterious Element** - Ancient runes, hidden door, cryptic message
3. **3+ Dialogue Lines** - With personality and subtext
4. **Puzzle/Strategy Challenge** - Decode pattern, outwit opponent
5. **Strong Hook/Cliffhanger** - Revelation, danger, plot twist

**Example (Age 11):**
```
The door looks ordinary enough—worn oak, tarnished brass handle, probably
older than your grandparents. Except. Except for the symbols carved into
the frame. They shimmer when you move your head, appearing and disappearing
like they can't quite decide if they want to be seen.

"That's weird," Spot whines, backing up a step. You've never heard him sound
nervous before.

"Tell me about it," you mutter, kneeling to examine the symbols. They're not
random scratches. There's a pattern here. Three symbols repeat: a circle, a
triangle, and something that might be a star or might be an eye. You count
them. Circle appears five times. Triangle, three times. Star-eye, once.

"5-3-1," you say aloud. "Or maybe..." You press the symbols in that order.
Nothing. Then backward: star, triangle-triangle-triangle, circle-circle...

The door *clicks*.

It swings inward on silent hinges, revealing darkness and the smell of old
books. Spot's ears flatten. From deep inside, you hear what might be footsteps.
Or wings.

Choices:
- Step through the door boldly ...calling out "Hello? Anyone there?"
- Send Spot in first to scout ...while you wait and listen
- Examine the door frame more carefully ...for traps or warnings before entering
```

**Why it works**: Clever (5-3-1 code), Mysterious (shimmer

ing symbols), Dialogue (Spot's nervous whine, "Tell me about it"), Puzzle (decode the symbols), Hook (footsteps or wings? Danger ahead)

### Ages 13-16: Depth Recipe (Wit, Complexity)

**Required Elements:**
1. **Sharp Detail** - Sarcastic observation, symbolic element, emotional insight
2. **Atmospheric/Thematic Element** - Moral gray area, identity question, power dynamics
3. **3+ Dialogue Lines** - With subtext, wit, or emotional weight
4. **Complex Challenge** - Moral dilemma, strategic puzzle, social navigation
5. **Compelling Hook** - Plot twist, character revelation, philosophical question

**Example (Age 15):**
```
The door's not locked. Of course it's not locked. That would be too easy, too
straightforward, too much like an actual obstacle instead of whatever test this
is supposed to be.

"You're stalling," Ash says from behind me, their voice flat. Not accusing, just
stating a fact.

"I'm thinking." I press my palm against the wood. It's warm. Warmer than it
should be. "Last time I went through a door without thinking, I ended up here.
Forgive me for being cautious."

"Caution and cowardice look pretty similar from where I'm standing."

I turn to face them. Ash's expression is unreadable—a skill they've perfected
in the three days since we've been trapped in this place. "And from where I'm
standing," I say carefully, "rushing in looks a lot like running away."

Their jaw tightens. Point to me.

Behind the door, something moves. Not footsteps. More like breathing. Large,
slow, deliberate breathing. The door handle is still cool to the touch, but
that warmth is spreading, creeping up my arm like an invitation. Or a warning.

I could open it. Face whatever's waiting. Or I could walk away, find another
path. But Ash is watching, and I'm tired of them thinking I'm afraid. Even if
I am.

Choices:
- Push through the door decisively ...proving to Ash (and yourself) you're brave enough
- Propose working together first ...suggesting you scout while Ash watches your back
- Call their bluff and walk away ...refusing to be manipulated by their taunts
```

**Why it works**: Sharp (sarcasm, psychological observation), Atmospheric (power dynamics, identity question "am I coward?"), Dialogue (subtext-heavy), Complex challenge (prove bravery vs strategic caution), Hook (what's breathing? Character conflict escalates)

### Ages 17+: Literary Recipe (Sophisticated, Immersive)

**Required Elements:**
1. **Evocative Detail** - Sensory-rich, metaphorical, psychologically revealing
2. **Thematic Depth** - Philosophical question, unreliable narration, existential stakes
3. **4+ Dialogue Lines** - Distinct voices, subtext, emotional resonance
4. **Meaningful Challenge** - Ethical dilemma, character-defining choice
5. **Powerful Hook** - Thematic revelation, transformation, emotional gut-punch

**Example (Adult):**
```
The door remembers me. I can tell by the way the wood grain shifts under my
fingertips, rearranging itself into patterns I recognize from childhood—my
mother's profile in the whorls, my father's smile in the knots. Memory made
manifest. Or manipulation. With this house, it's hard to tell the difference.

"It's not real," I say, but my voice lacks conviction.

"Of course it's not real." Mara's standing three steps behind me, far enough to
claim plausible deniability if things go wrong. She's been doing that a lot
lately—maintaining distance while pretending at solidarity. "Nothing in this
house is real. That's rather the point, isn't it?"

"The point," I say slowly, still tracing my mother's wooden face, "is that I'm
supposed to go through. That's what it wants."

"What *you* want."

"Don't."

"Avoidance doesn't suit you, love. Neither does self-deception." She moves
closer, and I catch her reflection in the door's brass plate—distorted, unfamiliar.
Or perhaps too familiar. "You came back here for a reason. To make peace with
what happened. To forgive them. To forgive yourself. You can't do that by
standing in the hallway having a philosophical crisis."

"What if I open it and they're there? Still disappointed. Still waiting for me
to be someone I'm not."

"Then you close it again." Her hand finds my shoulder, warm and steady. Real,
at least. "But you have to open it first."

The breathing on the other side has stopped. Replaced by something worse: the
sound of my own name, called in my mother's voice. Exactly as she said it the
last time I saw her. Before she died. Before I chose art over medicine, freedom
over her approval, myself over her ghost.

The door is a test. But whose? The house's, or mine?

Choices:
- Open the door and face whatever waits inside ...accepting that some conversations never end, even in death
- Ask Mara to come through with you ...refusing to carry this burden alone anymore
- Walk away from the door and the house entirely ...choosing to remember your mother as she was, not as this place wants to show her
- Demand the house show you the truth ...confronting it with your anger and your need to understand
```

**Why it works**: Evocative (sensory details, metaphor of wood grain as memory), Thematic (forgiveness, identity, parent expectations, choosing self vs approval), Dialogue (4 distinct exchanges with emotional weight, subtext), Meaningful challenge (face ghost vs move forward, ethical/emotional stakes), Powerful hook (mother's voice, unresolved grief, character transformation moment)

## 🎨 Vocabulary & Tone by Age

### Ages 3-5
- **Avoid**: parchment, depicts, constellations, nestled, vibrant, velvet
- **Use**: paper, shows, stars, sitting, bright, soft
- **Tone**: Simple, playful, reassuring

- **Avoid**: parchment, depicts, constellations, velvet, bustling, shimmering
- **Use**: paper, shows, stars, soft, busy, sparkling
- **Tone**: Playful but with more detail

### Ages 9-12
- **Encourage**: mysterious, ancient, glowing, whispered, twisted, echo, shadow
- **Avoid**: Textbook language, overly formal descriptions
- **Tone**: Vivid, engaging, not academic

### Ages 13-16
- **Encourage**: Sarcasm, wit, irony, subtext, complex emotions, atmospheric words (CAN use "constellations", "velvet", "parchment")
- **Tone**: Sophisticated but accessible, emotionally authentic

### Ages 17+
- **Encourage**: Full literary range—lyrical prose, sharp dialogue, rich metaphors
- **Avoid**: Pretentious, trying-too-hard language
- **Tone**: Literary but engaging, psychologically complex
