step-by-step with no clinical or legal background.
**Closes:** `docs/SEL_FRAMEWORK_ALIGNMENT.md` §5 (the `[TO BE NAMED]` placeholder) **and**
MT-266(c) (the clinical sign-off that gates the Adolescent antihero band) — **one engagement, two

**Tracked as:** MT-320.

---

## 1. What this is, in plain words

Right now the app's biggest competitor (Slumberkins) can say on their website: *"our content was
reviewed by [Name], LCSW."* We can't say anything like that yet.

Fixing it means: **find one licensed child-mental-health professional, pay them for roughly 4–8
hours of their time to read our SEL content, and get a short signed statement** saying the
content's developmental framing and safety language are sound. That's the whole task. It is a
small freelance gig for them — not a hire, not a retainer, not a business partner.

One engagement produces **two** things we need:

1. **The SEL attestation** — fills in `docs/SEL_FRAMEWORK_ALIGNMENT.md` §5, lets the marketing
   site and the parent-facing SEL page say "reviewed by [Name, credentials]."
2. **The antihero sign-off** — the review packet at
   `docs/CLINICAL_REVIEW_ADOLESCENT_ANTIHERO.md` is already prepared and waiting for exactly this
   person. Their sign-off is the launch gate for the Adolescent (15–17) band (MT-266c).

## 2. Who qualifies

Any ONE of the following, **currently licensed** (license number + state checkable online):

| Credential | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **LCSW** | Licensed Clinical Social Worker | Most common, most affordable; many specialize in children |
| **LMFT** | Licensed Marriage & Family Therapist | Fine if their practice focus is children/adolescents |
| **LPC / LPCC** | Licensed Professional (Clinical) Counselor | Same caveat — needs child/adolescent focus |
| **PsyD / PhD** | Licensed clinical or developmental psychologist | Stronger credential, higher rate |
| **School counselor (ASCA member)** | Only if *also* clinically licensed | ASCA membership alone is not a license |

**Must-haves:** (a) active license in any US state, (b) child and/or adolescent focus in their
actual practice, (c) willing to be **named publicly** with license type and state.

**Nice-to-haves:** experience with SEL curricula, bibliotherapy, or children's media review;
comfort reading a ~30-page packet.

**Not acceptable for this purpose:** life coaches, parenting coaches, "certified SEL
practitioners" without a clinical license, grad students, or anyone unwilling to be named.
The claim we want to make is only as strong as the license behind it.

## 3. What it costs

Typical child-therapist consulting rates run **$100–250/hour**. Budget for **4–8 hours**:
read the two documents + a sample of actual Life Quest / antihero content, one video call,
one written attestation.

**Realistic total: $500–1,500, one time.** Offer a flat fee, not hourly — e.g. **"$750 flat for
the review described in this packet"** — so neither side worries about the meter.

Ways to spend less (each is a real option, not a compromise on the license requirement):

- **University route:** email the child-clinical or school-psychology program director at a nearby
  university; faculty sometimes take small consulting engagements at lower rates, and being named
  advisor to an SEL product is CV-relevant for them.
- **Early-career licensee:** someone licensed 2–5 years is fully qualified for this review and
  typically charges the low end of the range.
- **Barter component:** a free Family-tier account for life is a fine sweetener, but the core fee
  should still be cash — an unpaid "advisor" who never actually reviewed anything is the exact
  credibility hole we're trying to close.

## 4. Where to find them (in order of least effort)

1. **Psychology Today therapist directory** (psychologytoday.com/us/therapists) — filter by
   *Child or Adolescent* + your state. Every profile shows license type. Email 5–10 whose
   profiles mention anxiety, school issues, or emotional regulation in kids.
2. **State NASW chapter** (for LCSWs) — most chapters have a consultation/referral list.
3. **Local university** — child-clinical psych or school-counseling faculty (see §3).
4. **Upwork / LinkedIn** — search "LCSW content review" / "child psychologist consultant."
   Verify the license yourself at the state licensing-board lookup site before engaging.
5. **Ask in the parenting/teacher communities** you're already joining for marketing (A6/A7 in
   `docs/MARKETING_PLAN.md`) — "can anyone recommend a child therapist who does content
   consulting?" is a normal ask there.

**License verification (do this for whoever you pick):** every state has a free public
license-lookup site (search "[state] LCSW license verification"). Confirm the license is
**active** and matches the name. Two minutes; do not skip.

## 6. What to send them once they say yes (the review packet)

1. `docs/SEL_FRAMEWORK_ALIGNMENT.md` — the CASEL/ASCA mapping they're attesting to (§1–§4).
2. `docs/CLINICAL_REVIEW_ADOLESCENT_ANTIHERO.md` — the prepared antihero review packet (MT-266c),
   including the evidence batch findings (7/7 being-seen arc; residual substance set-dressing risk).
3. **Content samples:** 2–3 Life Quests spanning bands (e.g. a Sprout Big Feelings quest,
   `standing_tall`, one boundary scenario) + 2–3 generated antihero issues from the evidence
   batch. Export as PDFs or paste into one doc — don't make them read source code.
4. The attestation template (§7 below).
5. A one-paragraph note on what the app is NOT: not therapy, not a diagnostic tool, positioned as
   "supports SEL" never "treats" — so their statement can echo that boundary.

## 7. The attestation template (what they sign)

Two short statements — they can edit the wording, but each must keep the four properties from
`SEL_FRAMEWORK_ALIGNMENT.md` §5: named credentials, stated scope, dated specificity, and the
not-therapy boundary.

**(a) SEL alignment attestation** — goes into `SEL_FRAMEWORK_ALIGNMENT.md` §5 and may be quoted
on the website:

> "I, [Name], [license type, state, license #], reviewed the CASEL 5 and ASCA framework mapping
> in *SEL Framework Alignment — Once Upon YOUR Child* and a sample of the app's Life Quest and
> boundary-setting content on [date]. I found the developmental framing, age-band
> appropriateness, and safety language consistent with accepted social-emotional-learning
> practice for the stated age ranges. This review is a content and design review; it does not
> constitute a clinical trial, outcome validation, or endorsement by any licensing or framework
> organization. This product is not therapy and does not replace care from a mental-health
> professional."

**(b) Adolescent antihero sign-off** — the MT-266c launch gate; scope per
`docs/CLINICAL_REVIEW_ADOLESCENT_ANTIHERO.md` (their conclusion may be "approved,"
"approved with changes," or "not approved" — an honest "with changes" is a good outcome, not a
failure; fold the changes in and get a one-line re-confirm).
