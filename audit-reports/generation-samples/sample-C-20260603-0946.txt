The Last Star of Comet Street
=============================

[Page 1]
A knot tightened in Mateo’s stomach, small and hard like a pebble. This was it. The last day on Comet Street. The house, usually bustling with the sounds of home, now echoed with the harsh squeak of packing tape and the hushed voices of his parents. Everywhere he looked, boxes stood like silent, cardboard sentinels, swallowing his world piece by piece. Mateo traced the worn pattern of the living room rug with his bare foot, feeling the late-summer dust that settled on everything, a thin layer covering memories. He couldn't shake the heavy feeling that he was leaving more than just a house; he was leaving the only sky he'd ever known for stargazing.

[Page 2]
Mateo’s gaze drifted to the ceiling, where a faded glow-in-the-dark star used to shine brightest. He’d put it there years ago, the 'North Star' of his bedroom ceiling, a beacon for his dreams. But now, it was gone. His heart gave a lurch. Had it been packed? Thrown away? He felt a quick pang of something like grief. That star wasn't just plastic; it was a map to all his imaginary voyages. "My North Star," he whispered, a cloud of worry dimming his usual bright thoughts. From his shoulder, Rockin' Robin let out a soft, inquiring whistle, her head cocked. She darted off, a blur of bright feathers, surveying the empty room from the top of a tall stack of boxes. Her keen eyes missed nothing.

[Page 3]
Robin's mission was always to scout, to find the clearest path, and she took it seriously. She chirped once, a short, clear note, then zipped to the hallway, her tiny wings a whir. Mateo followed, his sneakers squeaking on the bare floorboards. The smell of empty rooms, of old wood and forgotten secrets, filled the air. Robin landed on a bookshelf, now stripped bare. Three sharp chirps burst from her, loud and insistent, signaling danger. Mateo froze. A shadow, long and thin, stretched across the floor from under the shelf. Robin launched herself wings-first, a brave, tiny warrior, trying to distract the 'threat.' But it was just a discarded piece of packing tape, fluttering in a faint draft. Robin, ever quick to act, had been wrong again.

[Page 4]
Mateo chuckled softly, bending to pick up the tape. "Just tape, Robin," he said, extending his finger for her to land. She landed on his shoulder, ruffling her feathers, a tiny feather from her chest now stuck to Mateo’s shirt. He gently brushed it away. His gaze swept the hallway, and then he noticed something small and silver tucked under the baseboard heater, half-hidden. It was a tiny plastic astronaut, one he used to carry everywhere. It wasn’t his North Star, but it was a piece of his old universe. A flicker of an idea sparked in his mind: if this was here, maybe his star was in his old fort, the one under the stairs, a place even his parents had forgotten.

[Page 5]
The fort under the stairs. Mateo remembered the complicated system of blankets and pillows, the hidden entrance only he knew. But that was years ago. He pushed open the small, creaking door to the closet under the staircase. Inside, it was dark and dusty, a tangled mess of forgotten toys and old winter coats. This wasn’t the perfect, cozy fort he remembered; it was just a closet. Mateo felt another wave of disappointment. The fort, like his house, was irrevocably changed. Had he imagined it all? Robin let out a long, clear note, signaling that this space, at least, was safe. Mateo took a deep breath. His heart sunk, wondering if he should even bother looking for the star here. His mom called from downstairs, "Mateo! Just ten minutes until we leave!"

[Page 6]
Mateo had to make a choice: give up and leave, or risk not finding his star. But finding it meant crawling into that dusty space, maybe making more of a mess. He closed his eyes for a moment, picturing the old fort, how he used to navigate its nooks and crannies. He remembered a secret pocket he’d sewn into a blanket, meant for his most treasured possessions. What if the North Star was still there? Robin, sensing his hesitation, hopped onto his head, her tiny feet light on his hair. Mateo squinted into the darkness. He took a deep, steadying breath. He would just have to make a new memory of the fort, not the old one.

[Page 7]
Mateo squeezed into the fort, his hands brushing away cobwebs. He felt like an astronaut exploring a new, forgotten galaxy. He found the old blanket, crusty with dust, and his fingers fumbled for the secret pocket. His heart pounded. There! A small, flat object. He pulled it out, bringing it into the dim light. It was his North Star, still faintly glowing! But it wasn't alone. Tucked beside it was a tiny, crumpled drawing of Comet Street, with Mateo and his friends waving. And another drawing, too small for him to have made: a crude stick figure of a bird, with the words, 'Robin’s Nest.' Robin, who had been perched silently on his shoulder, let out a soft, happy trill, then rubbed her head against his cheek. She’d known it was here, perhaps, or hoped it was.

[Page 8]
Mateo carefully tucked the glow-in-the-dark star and the tiny drawings into his pocket. He might be leaving Comet Street, but he wasn’t leaving behind his imagination or the loyalty of his smallest friend. He emerged from the fort, dust clinging to his clothes but a newfound lightness in his chest. His old fort wasn't what it used to be, but it had given him something new: a last, secret treasure, a reminder that even when things change, you can find a piece of yourself, or a new beginning, in the most unexpected places. As his mom called him for the final time, Mateo turned to leave, Rockin' Robin settled happily on his shoulder, a tiny, vibrant feather now tucked into his hand.
