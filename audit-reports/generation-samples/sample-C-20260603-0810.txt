The Last Star on Comet Street
=============================

[Page 1]
Mateo’s chest felt tight. The sound of packing tape squealed from downstairs, a high-pitched protest like a trapped mouse. Sunlight, thick with late-summer dust, streamed through his bedroom window, illuminating dancing motes where his fort-building supplies used to be. Every corner of the empty room echoed a single, overwhelming thought: he was leaving Comet Street. He’d never known another home, and the idea of a new town, new streets, new everything, made his stomach flutter.

[Page 2]
A soft, melodic whistle, like a warm morning breeze, sounded close. Rockin’ Robin, a flash of blue and gold, landed on Mateo’s shoulder. She tilted her head, her bright eyes seeming to understand his silent worry. Mateo reached up, gently stroking her soft feathers. He thought of his fort, the “Star-Seeker’s Stronghold,” hidden deep in the woods behind his house. He had to see it one last time, just to say goodbye.

[Page 3]
Mateo slipped out the back door, Robin darting ahead. She flew low, scanning the familiar path. Suddenly, three sharp chirps cut through the quiet air. Stop! Mateo froze, his heart thumping. Robin landed on a low branch, wings spread, ready. He crept forward cautiously, peering through the undergrowth. His stomach dropped like a stone.

[Page 4]
There, where his magnificent fort once stood, was nothing but a scatter of old, mossy boards. Weeds had swallowed the carefully cleared ground. It was no longer the Star-Seeker’s Stronghold, just a forgotten pile of wood. A heavy wave of sadness washed over Mateo. It was truly gone. He knelt, tracing the rough grain of a fallen plank. This felt like the real goodbye to Comet Street, not the moving truck.

[Page 5]
As Mateo sat, Robin suddenly launched herself, wings beating fast and loud, directly at a chattering squirrel that had scampered too close. The squirrel shrieked and disappeared up a tree. Robin, fearless and quick, landed back on Mateo’s shoulder, dropping a small, grey feather onto his knee. Mateo managed a weak smile. He ran his hand over the old wood again, and his fingers brushed against something hard and smooth beneath a loose board.

[Page 6]
It was the Comet Stone. Mateo gasped. He and his best friend, Leo, had found it years ago, imagining it had fallen from space. They’d kept it hidden in the fort, a secret treasure. The fort was gone, changed irrevocably, but the Comet Stone, a symbol of their friendship and shared imagination, remained. He carefully picked it up, tucking it into his pocket. The ache in his chest lessened, replaced by a quiet warmth. He would take this piece of Comet Street with him.

[Page 7]
As Mateo rose, he noticed a corner of something papery under the same loose board. He pulled it out. It was a tattered map, drawn in crayon, to “The Whispering Star-Oak.” His friends' messy handwriting was unmistakable. Robin let out one long, clear note – safe! Mateo’s imagination flared. The Star-Oak was their secret stargazing spot. Had his friends left a final message there, knowing he was leaving? His loyalty pulsed, urging him to find out.

[Page 8]
Mateo set off, following the faded lines of the map. The familiar paths felt different now, each fallen leaf and sun-dappled root seeming to whisper goodbyes. Late-summer dust puffed up around his sneakers with every step. Robin flew ahead, her keen eyes scanning. Three sharp chirps! She swooped low, launching herself towards a grumpy-looking terrier tied near a garden gate. Its barks turned into confused yelps as Robin expertly distracted it, buying Mateo just enough time to slip past.

[Page 9]
Finally, the Whispering Star-Oak stood before them. Mateo gasped again. A massive branch, thicker than his body, had broken off the ancient tree, lying sprawled across their usual stargazing spot. The clearing felt open, exposed, no longer the secret, cozy haven he remembered. Another wave of disappointment washed over him. Even this special place had changed, almost beyond recognition. It wasn't fair.

[Page 10]
Robin nudged his ear with her beak. Mateo looked up at the remaining branches, then at the missing branch. He realized the break wasn't destruction. Instead, it opened up a whole new section of the night sky, a view they’d never had before. A different kind of secret. As he scanned the trunk, a tiny, faded carving caught his eye, higher up than he’d ever looked. It was a comet, followed by three small, faint stars. An old, hidden message, winking back at him from the bark.

[Page 11]
Mateo reached up, gently tracing the comet carving. He pulled out the Comet Stone from his pocket. He understood. Comet Street, the Comet Stone, the comet on the tree. It was all connected. He didn't need to take the stone with him to keep the memory. He carefully placed the smooth, grey Comet Stone at the base of the Star-Oak, right next to the carving. A silent goodbye to Comet Street, and a quiet hello to whatever new wonders awaited him.

[Page 12]
Just then, he heard his mother’s voice calling his name, echoing faintly through the woods. It was time. Robin landed on his shoulder, dropping a shimmering, iridescent beetle shell into his hand. Mateo hurried home. His room was utterly bare now, stripped of everything. The clean, vast smell of an empty room hung in the air. He looked out the window one last time. The sun was setting, painting Comet Street in oranges and purples. A single star pricked the darkening sky. He held the tiny, glowing beetle shell in his palm. A new kind of adventure, he thought, a brand new sky waiting to be explored. He felt a quiet sense of peace.
