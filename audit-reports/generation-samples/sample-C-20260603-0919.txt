The Last Star of Comet Street
=============================

[Page 1]
Mateo’s stomach felt like a tangled ball of yarn. It was the last day on Comet Street. Every sound in the house was a reminder: the sharp *squeak-rip* of packing tape, the hollow echo of his own voice. He wandered into his bedroom, where bare walls already seemed to breathe a different, empty air. The familiar smell of his room, usually a mix of old books and summer grass, was now just a faint whiff of late-summer dust. He gripped a small, smooth river stone in his pocket, a comforting weight. It was from the creek behind his house, a creek he might never see again. Mateo sighed, a puff of air that stirred the dust motes dancing in the afternoon sun.

[Page 2]
“Mateo! Time for lunch!” his dad called from downstairs. Mateo didn’t answer. He had one last mission. His secret fort, nestled deep in the backyard bushes, held his most precious treasure: the Stardust Jar. It wasn't real stardust, of course, just glitter he’d collected, but to Mateo, it was the magic of every starry night he’d ever watched from Comet Street. He needed it for his new room, in the new town. As he thought this, a quick, melodic whistle sounded just outside his window. A flash of blue and grey, and Rockin' Robin landed gracefully on his shoulder, a tiny, bright red berry clutched in her beak.

[Page 3]
“Hey, Robin,” Mateo whispered, taking the berry. Robin hopped, her small feet tickling his neck. She gave another soft whistle, then darted out the open window, a blur of motion. Mateo followed, his mission renewed. The backyard felt different too, not quite his anymore. The once-lush path to the fort was now littered with stray bits of packing peanuts and flattened cardboard boxes. His fort, usually a perfect hideout of woven branches and old blankets, looked saggy and tired, half-hidden under a pile of discarded moving supplies. It made Mateo’s heart sink. How could he get to the Stardust Jar now?

[Page 4]
Robin zipped ahead, circling the fort. Suddenly, she let out three sharp chirps. *Stop. Wait.* Mateo froze. He heard a rustle from behind the fort. Was it a neighbor? A forgotten moving helper? Robin, true to form, didn’t hesitate. With a loud, fearless squawk, she launched herself wings-first towards the rustling, creating a flurry of feathers and noise. Mateo watched as a startled squirrel, halfway to burying an acorn, leaped onto a fence and chittered angrily at Robin before scampering away. Robin, having successfully 'distracted' the 'threat,' landed back on Mateo's shoulder, panting slightly.

[Page 5]
“Good job, Robin,” Mateo chuckled, even though his heart still gave a little flutter. The direct path was still blocked by a towering stack of plastic bins. But Mateo, with his builder’s eye, noticed something else. A small gap in the bushes, usually too small for him, but now a bit wider where the fort had shifted. It was a route he’d never taken before, an adventurer’s way. He crouched low, pushing aside damp leaves and spiderwebs. Robin flew just above his head, guiding him with soft, reassuring chirps. The air grew cooler and smelled of damp earth as he squeezed through, emerging inside the fort’s main chamber.

[Page 6]
His fort felt different inside, smaller and colder without the familiar warmth of his blankets. He looked for the secret shelf where the Stardust Jar always sat, tucked away safely. But the shelf was bare. The jar was gone. A heavy knot tightened in Mateo’s chest, much worse than the one from earlier. He’d lost it. His special Comet Street magic. He sat down on the dusty ground, feeling the rough texture of old twigs poke through his jeans. Robin landed gently beside him, nudging his hand with her beak, as if sensing his sadness.

[Page 7]
Mateo brushed a tear from his eye, stirring up more late-summer dust. Then, something caught his eye. Right where the Stardust Jar used to be, a small, smooth pebble lay glinting faintly. It wasn't the river stone from his pocket; this one shimmered with an inner light, a soft, ethereal glow, like a captured sliver of moonlight. He picked it up. It was warm to the touch. It wasn’t the Stardust Jar, but it felt special, almost magical. He remembered the times he and his friends would look for unique stones and call them 'star fragments.' Maybe this was a real one.

[Page 8]
Above him, the fort's roof, which had always been a solid canopy, now had a new, jagged tear from where a branch had fallen. Through the gap, Mateo saw a pinprick of pure white light. It was the first star of the evening, clear and bright in the deepening twilight sky. It seemed to wink at him. He held the luminous pebble, its faint glow mimicking the star above. A new idea sparked in Mateo’s mind, shining just as brightly as the star. What if the Stardust Jar wasn't about the glitter, but about the *feeling* of wonder, the act of looking up?

[Page 9]
Mateo stood up, a new purpose filling him. He could build a new fort in his new room, even better, with a window that faced the night sky. He would put this glowing pebble, his real star fragment, on his windowsill. Every night, it would remind him of Comet Street, of Robin, and of the endless stars. Robin gave a long, clear note, a sound that meant *safe, all clear.* Mateo carefully tucked the pebble into his pocket, beside his river stone. It felt like carrying a piece of the sky with him. He knew he wouldn't be leaving the magic of Comet Street behind after all. He gave the old fort one last glance, then headed for home, ready to face the quiet house.
