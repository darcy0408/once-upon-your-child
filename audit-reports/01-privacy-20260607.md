Date: 2026-06-07

Scope: "Once Upon YOUR Child" / Story Weaver — Flutter client, Flask API,
Celery worker, Postgres on Railway, Isar/SharedPreferences/flutter_secure_storage
local stores, and third-party processors (Google Gemini, OpenRouter, Replicate,
Cloudflare Workers AI, ElevenLabs, Stripe, Sentry, Firebase/Google Analytics,

## Executive Summary

The architecture shows real, careful COPPA engineering — a server-side
`ConsentRecord` with a hashed, rate-limited, fail-closed email round-trip; a
parental math-gate before the consent action; analytics and Sentry hard-gated
off for under-13; a centralized erasure cascade shared by the user-initiated and
scheduled-purge paths; and a disciplined real-name scrub before prompts leave
for any AI provider. The mechanisms to be compliant largely exist. The problem
is that the single most important one is switched off, and the erasure cascade
has holes.

There are three launch Blockers.

First and decisive: the under-13 COPPA verifiable-consent round-trip is disabled
in every build, including release. `_kSkipEmailConsent = true`
(`lib/screens/parental_consent_screen.dart:23`) forces every under-13 child onto
the same self-attested checkbox as a teen; `welcome_screen.dart:872` grants the
child full access on that checkbox alone. A self-attested checkbox is the textbook
example of a method that does NOT satisfy COPPA §312.5(b). As shipped, the app
collects personal information from children under 13 without verifiable parental
consent (PRIV-01).

Second: the "Delete All My Data" erasure does not actually erase all the child's
data. `purge_user_data` (`backend/services/data_retention.py:91`) never deletes
`ParentHiddenContext` — the parent-authored Big Feelings record (trigger, coping
tool, repair goal) tied to the child — nor `ConsentVerificationCode` rows, and
the client never clears local SharedPreferences/Isar. Sensitive emotional-context
data survives a right-to-erasure request (PRIV-04), violating COPPA §312.7 and
GDPR Art. 17.

Third: erasure does not propagate to Stripe. `purge_user_data` nulls
`stripe_customer_id` locally (`data_retention.py:152`) but never calls Stripe to
delete the customer, so the parent's payment identity persists at a sub-processor
after deletion (PRIV-05) — GDPR Art. 17(2) / CCPA §1798.105(c).

Beyond the Blockers: a child's IP address and user_id persist in `AuditLog` after
erasure with no purge job (PRIV-09); GDPR-K Art. 8 age-of-consent for the EU
(13–16 by member state) is never applied — the gate is a flat US-13 line
(PRIV-06); a `debug_bypass` consent method is accepted by the production consent
endpoint (PRIV-07); and the daily retention purge depends on Celery-beat liveness
that the observability audit flagged as potentially silent. Sentry PII posture
is strong (validated by the observability audit) but the worker still logs a full
pre-scrub prompt that can contain a child's real name (PRIV-08, framed here as a
data-protection issue). No third-party advertising SDK and no geolocation/biometric
profiling of under-13 were found — the two absolute §312.5 restrictions are clear.

## VPC Audit (COPPA §312.5)

What the app actually does for an under-13 user, as shipped:

1. Age picker → if age < 13, the child is routed to `ParentalConsentScreen`
   (`welcome_screen.dart:863`).
2. A parental math-gate (`ParentalGateDialog`) must be cleared before the notice
   and consent action appear (`parental_consent_screen.dart:611`) — a reasonable
   §312.5(b) "is this a parent" effort.
3. Because `_kSkipEmailConsent = true` (`:23`), `_requiresEmailVerification` is
   false (`:82`), so `_submitConsent` (`:864`) takes the 13+ branch and calls
   `recordConsent(method: 'self_attested', verified: false)` (`:873`), then pops
   `true`. The child is granted full access on a checkbox.

A self-attested checkbox is explicitly insufficient under §312.5(b). The FTC-
recognized methods (credit-card/payment transaction, signed form, video, knowledge-
based, government-ID match) are not exercised on the live path. The email round-
trip the codebase implements (`user_routes.py:432-748`) is a defensible
"email-plus" method, but it is dead code while the flag is set.

Verdict: INSUFFICIENT — Blocker (PRIV-01). The fix is a one-character flip plus a
Resend key (the round-trip is built and fails closed: `user_routes.py:463`).

`debug_bypass` is also accepted by the production `record_consent` endpoint's
`ALLOWED_CONSENT_METHODS` (`user_routes.py:34-40`) — it is only reachable from the
client via a compile-time `--dart-define` flag, but the server should not treat a
"bypass" method as a valid consent record in production (PRIV-07).

§312.4 direct notice: STRONG. The Notice to Parents
(`parental_consent_screen.dart:210-371`) names operator, postal address, phone,
email, every sub-processor, what is collected, and what is not done — and the same
content appears in `PRIVACY_POLICY.md`. This satisfies §312.4(d) content.

§312.7 access/deletion: PARTIAL. Access (`GET /api/user/<id>/export`,
`user_routes.py:335`) and deletion (`DELETE /api/user/<id>/data`,
`user_routes.py:292`) endpoints exist and are owner-gated. Deletion is incomplete
(PRIV-04, PRIV-05, PRIV-09).

## Policy Alignment (PRIVACY_POLICY.md vs code reality)

- ALIGNED: sub-processor list, photo "sent to servers, not stored," operator
  identity/address/phone, "no advertising," analytics off by default and not for
  under-13, big-feelings framed as transient personalization.
- MISALIGNED: policy line 91 promises "verifiable consent before any personal
  data is collected (COPPA)"; the live build collects under-13 data on a checkbox
  (PRIV-01). Policy line 102 promises deletion "permanently deletes your child's
  stories, characters and profile data"; ParentHiddenContext, local store, and
  Stripe identity survive (PRIV-03/04/05).
- STALE DOC: `docs/COPPA_AUDIT.md:18` and `:54` assert photos are "processed
  on-device" / "NOT uploaded." False — `backend/routes/avatar_routes.py:275`
  uploads the photo to the server (PRIV-11). The current PRIVACY_POLICY.md is
  correct; the older audit doc is not.
- POLICY GAP: international-transfer section claims SCCs/adequacy generically;
  EU child data also needs the Art. 8 age-of-consent handling that the code does
  not implement (PRIV-06).
