# UX / Product Audit 09 — Heuristic + Funnel Analysis

**Date:** 2026-05-31
**Method:** Expert heuristic review (Nielsen's 10 + kids-app additions: parental gate, age-appropriate language, recoverable errors) plus a conversion-funnel pass. Code/design review only; no real-user testing.
**Scope:** discovery to first completed story to return — acquisition, age gate, parental consent, wizard, AI generation, story display, save/library, settings, payment, instrumentation.
**Coverage:** install, signup, consent, wizard, generation, display, settings, payment all audited. 58 findings (4 Critical, 14 High, 24 Medium, 16 Low). 34 reading-level samples across parent and child surfaces.
**Companion artifact:** `09-ux-product-20260531-funnel-map.csv`

---

## Executive Summary

Story Weaver's experience is, screen-for-screen, strong: the onboarding teaser, the generation wait-state (animation + rotating copy + sparkle mini-game), the Sprout wizard, and the storybook reader are above the bar for kids apps. The problems are not craft problems — they are **correctness, consistency, and measurement** problems concentrated at the two points where money and legal duty live: the under-13 consent gate and the paywall.

Four findings are Critical. Two are launch-blockers. **A-01:** verifiable COPPA consent is disabled in all builds (`_kSkipEmailConsent = true`) — every under-13 child is currently onboarded via self-attestation, which is not verifiable consent. **A-02:** the "12-14" age bucket resolves to age 12, so honest 13- and 14-year-olds are routed into the under-13 consent path — a single tappable control straddles the COPPA boundary. **P-01:** three contradictory price tables coexist (paywall says Premium $4.99 / Family $9.99; the canonical `TierPricing` and `subscription_screen` say $9.99 / $19.99) — a parent can be quoted one price and charged another, a hidden-cost/bait-and-switch failure. **P-02:** the purchase and subscription-start analytics methods exist but have zero call sites — revenue and conversion are completely invisible.

The funnel's structural weakness is instrumentation. Outside a couple of Hero-Creator and grace-banner events, the conversion funnel is uninstrumented: paywall-view, plan-select, checkout-start, purchase-success, and every BYOK step are absent or defined-but-dead. Worse, the analytics layer is a no-op on web (the production `grand-light` frontend), so even wired events will not fire on the primary platform. You cannot today answer "what percent of paywall views convert" or "does BYOK convert better than paid."

The good news: most fixes are correctness edits, not redesigns. None require weakening the parental gate. The highest-leverage work is a pricing single-source-of-truth, re-enabling verifiable consent, wiring four already-defined analytics methods, and adding a web-safe analytics sink.

## 4. Drop-Off Candidates

Ranked by projected impact on overall conversion (parent reaching a satisfying first story and returning):

1. **Under-13 parental consent (30-40%).** Structurally the heaviest gate and legally required to be heavy — but A-01 (no verifiable consent) and A-06 (scroll-gate traps) make it both non-compliant and avoidably frustrating. Do not thin below COPPA threshold; optimize the math gate, scroll mechanics, and the one child-facing string at grade 5-6.
2. **Plan selection + checkout (40-50%).** P-01's conflicting prices are a trust-destroying defect at the exact moment of payment. The "example" `SubscriptionScreen` (P-06) and missing renewal terms (P-15) compound it.
3. **Empty-state library dead-end (25-35%).** D-09 — a first-time user who reaches an empty library has no path forward. Single-CTA fix, high impact.
4. **Generation error recovery (30-40% of error-hitters).** D-03 surfaces raw exceptions to children and offers no fallback; this converts a transient backend blip into an abandonment.
5. **Wizard choice overload — Adventure Team & Story Type (15-22%).** W-04/W-05 — the two highest-load screens for a child; progressive disclosure recovers them.
6. **Generation wait on slow networks (12-20%).** D-01/D-02 — the fake progress that says "Almost ready!" for 30s is the one thing that breaks the otherwise excellent wait-state.

**Conflicting-heuristic tradeoffs (documented, not resolved):**
- *Visibility vs simplicity at the age gate:* a stronger parent-zone signal (A-08) adds visual weight to a screen we want to feel light for the child. Resolve toward visibility — the parent must know this control is theirs.
- *COPPA friction vs conversion:* the under-13 gate intentionally trades conversion for compliance. The only levers are non-legal copy and mechanics; the gate itself must stay.
- *Emotional delight vs honest status during generation:* the sparkle game and fake progress make the wait feel shorter but mislead on a slow network. Resolve toward honesty (D-01) while keeping the game.

## 5. Instrumentation Plan

**The central finding: the conversion funnel is effectively uninstrumented, and the analytics layer is a no-op on the production web platform.** `FirebaseAnalyticsService.logEvent` no-ops until consent (`firebase_analytics_service.dart:88-89`) and bails on web; `OnboardingAnalytics` bails on web entirely (`onboarding_analytics.dart:9`). Since the production `grand-light` frontend is Flutter Web, **most funnel events will not fire on the primary platform even once wired.** Resolving a web-safe analytics sink (or backend event logging) is the single highest-value instrumentation task and gates the value of everything below.

## 6. Recommendations

**P0 — launch blockers (do before any public release):**
